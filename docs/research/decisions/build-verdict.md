# Verdict: Build It, But Not As An All-In-One

**Decided 2026-09-06**, immediately after the international scan in [`../market/deck-scan.md`](../market/deck-scan.md)
found that four of the five slide-deck pages are occupied and that the round-trip claim is damaged.

**The question asked.** Given that all of this already exists, should we drop the idea? Or is combining the pieces into
one platform itself the unique thing?

**The answer.** Build it. And no, the combination is not the unique thing, and claiming it is would cost us more than
the competitors do.

---

## Why "It Already Exists" Does Not Disqualify Us

**Nothing in the rules cares that competitors exist.** The disqualification list covers plagiarism, uncredited IP,
undeclared external code, pre-release work and missed deadlines. Building a trip planner that resembles Wanderlog is
none of those.

**And we are not being scored against Wanderlog.** The organisers said there are "200 groups, I think 200 plus" and
ten will be selected, from two problem statements. Roughly a hundred teams are building a travel planner from the same
brief we are. **The comparison a judge actually makes is against those hundred**, not against a funded product with
eleven million points of interest. Mindtrip beats us. That is not the contest.

## The Arithmetic That Settles It

Originality is **7 marks**. If the round-trip damage drops us from the 4-5 band to the 2-3 band, that is roughly
**4 marks lost**.

Now the other column, as of today:

| Band                                  | Marks | State                                     |
| ------------------------------------- | ----: | ----------------------------------------- |
| Visual diagrams and mindmaps          |     8 | Empty canvas, blank index                 |
| Mentor consultation                   |     7 | `sessions/` is empty                      |
| Breadth of exploration                |     3 | One idea logged                           |
| Target group alignment                |     5 | "Travellers", which is the 2-3 band       |
| Effectiveness                         |     7 | Judged against a user we have not named   |
| **At or near zero, or at risk**       | **30** |                                          |

**Thirty marks are sitting unclaimed and four are at risk.** Worrying about the four while the thirty go unwritten is
optimising the wrong variable. None of the thirty requires the idea to be original. They require a drawn diagram, two
booked calls, a named person and a few alternatives compared.

## Why "All-In-One" Is The Weakest Claim We Could Make

Three reasons, and each of them costs marks.

1. **It is the most common thing a hackathon team says.** One judge has judged fifteen hackathons. "We combine
   everything into one platform" is the sentence they have heard most often, and it is a Creativity answer that reads
   as an absence of one.
2. **The incumbents already own that position.** Mindtrip is a conversational planner with maps, day-by-day
   itineraries, collaboration, booking and eleven million points of interest. Wanderlog has map, itinerary, budget,
   collaboration, offline and AI. If breadth is the claim, we lose to them on the claim's own terms.
3. **Feasibility punishes it directly.** Scope realism is 5 marks and resource and time awareness is 4. The deck's five
   pages need four data sources we do not have: place data, photo-spot data, cultural-caution data and a news feed.
   Four people, three weeks. **Each thin page we add makes a judge's comparison worse, not better** - our photo page
   against PhotoHound is a fight we lose in the ten seconds it takes to open both.

**Breadth of features is a liability under this rubric, not an asset.**

## What We Claim Instead

Not a feature count. One mechanism, in one sentence a judge can repeat:

> **The itinerary knows what can break it, and repairs itself from options the group already approved.**

The group votes. The vote produces the trip **and** a ranked bench of everything that lost. When a stop closes, the
bench is the replacement pool, so no new vote is needed. When the trip goes over budget, the same bench is the cut
list.

**Why this survives the scan.** Sitata alerts against your exact itinerary but does not re-plan it. Troupe and
WePlanify vote but do nothing when a plan breaks. Contour chooses a loop shape but has no group and no places. Nothing
found closes the whole loop. **And it is what the problem statement literally asks for** - "re-planning when something
changes mid-trip" - so it is the brief, not a bolt-on.

The round-trip choice stays as a **secondary** twist, stated in the narrow form the corrected
[`../market/landscape.md`](../market/landscape.md) records. It is worth marks under Novel Features. It is no longer the
headline.

## What This Changes About The Deck Mockup

The deck dropped the vote, the bench and the substitution, which are now the entire argument. So:

- **Put the mechanism back.** The deck needs the vote and the bench, or it is a pretty itinerary with no claim
- **Fuse pages 3 and 4 into the itinerary rather than leaving them beside it.** A caution attached to the stop it
  applies to is the one seam the scan could not fill. A caution on its own page is a worse Travel Smart App
- **Demote photo spots** to a detail on a stop, if it stays at all. Never a headline, never a pitched feature
- **Keep the Google Maps handoff** and pitch it under Feasibility

## What Would Change This Verdict

If someone opens **Troupe** and finds it already turns losing votes into a replacement pool when a plan breaks, then
both claims are gone and the idea needs rethinking, not repositioning. That check is
`[not verified]` and it is the single most valuable half-hour available right now.
