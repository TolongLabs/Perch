1 · Landing

Public. The only job is to make someone understand the mechanism in one sentence and press one button.

Behaviour

  • Two entry points, side by side. "Start A Storybook" and "Paste A Post" are peers. The post route is the second front door, not a feature buried inside onboarding.
  • The hero shows the book mid-flip. A still of a finished spread reads as a template; a flip reads as a product.
  • The three cards are the mechanism in order — propose, veto, repair. Not feature bullets.
Open: does a landing page belong in the prototype at all? It is not part of the core flow, and Mockup Completeness scores the flow end to end. Cheap to keep, cheap to cut.

Behaviour

  • Question 3 is the whole reason the interview exists. Three chosen out of nine is a ranking, and that ranking is the bench. Every other question shapes what the nine are.
  • The rail is progress, summary and edit affordance in one. Tapping a completed answer jumps back to it. Answers accumulate visibly so the profile is seen forming.
  • An escape hatch on every step. "Say it in my own words" feeds the same slot as the taps — free text is a first-class answer, not a fallback.
  • The five steps are Who's coming · What it's for · Must-see · How you travel · Non-negotiables. Question 1 also decides whether the planner shows vote counts or just picks.
Open: the conversational bubble and a voice toggle are in the reference but unjustified here. A voice mode nobody demos is a screen that costs design time and earns nothing.

Behaviour

  • Length is an output, not a slider. The date strip takes everyone's availability and the overlap decides how many days the trip is. Already built in the deployed deck.
  • Transport is pre-filled from the interview, not asked cold. "Friends, dawn to midnight" implies self-drive; the user only corrects it.
  • Looking like a form is the point. Making this feel like part of the interview is what produced the eight-question booking form we rejected.

7 · Generating

The only place a wait is acceptable, so it should earn it.

Building your trip

Behaviour

  • Name the work, not a spinner. Listing the actual steps is what makes the agent legible — and it is the only moment the user sees the tools running.
  • It resolves into the planner, not the book. In shape B nothing is finished yet, so landing on a book here would be a lie.

Behaviour

  • Asynchronous by default. "Waiting on 1", "Nudge the group", avatars in the header. Votes land over days, not in one sitting — that is the whole justification for this being a separate surface from the interview.
  • Ties are a real state and need a real answer. Two of four each. Who breaks it — the trip creator, the earliest vote, or the agent on cost? Currently unanswered.
  • Settled days collapse. The surface should shrink as the trip converges, so the open decision is always the biggest thing on screen.
  • Finalise stays locked until every day is settled. That lock is what makes the book a finished object.
This screen carries shape B. If it ends up being something people pass through in one sitting, it is interview-part-two with a progress bar and the fork deserves reopening. Notifications, presence and the nudge are what make it a room instead of a step.

Behaviour

  • Stops are not tappable. This is output. "Edit Plan" is the only way back, and it goes to the planner — that is the cost of shape B, accepted knowingly.
  • Share and Download are what make it a keepsake, and they are the argument that won B. A public share link and a PDF are the reasons a finished book beats an editable one.

Open: real page-flip or a spread that cross-fades? A true flip is a canvas effect and has to run on the deployed link, in the video, at demo scale. If it will not, a clean spread beats a janky flip.

10 · Something Broke

The headline claim. In shape B the book cannot repair itself, so this path has to be fast enough that the claim survives.

Day 3 no longer works
Merapi jeep operators suspended for haze until Sunday. Merapi museum was your number two for that slot, and it keeps Day 3 at RM 40.

Then, on the planner
Day 3 · Morningreopened

Behaviour

  • "Use The Bench" is a one-tap accept that skips the planner entirely. Without it, B's repair is four steps and the claim dies in the demo. With it, B gets most of A's immediacy back.
  • The one-sentence explanation is the product. What broke, what replaced it, why that one, and what it costs — in a sentence a judge can repeat.
  • Agent tool call #2 lives here: opening hours, weather, then a ranked pick off the bench.
  • Reaching the group: notification, or email, or both. Whoever taps first resolves it for everyone.
Biggest open question in the app. If a closure needs group consent, "Use The Bench" needs a rule — creator decides, first responder decides, or it auto-applies and the group can undo. Auto-apply-with-undo is the only version that demos in one shot.
