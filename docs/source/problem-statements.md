### Beating The Burnout

**Lifestyle Track: Beating The Burnout**

**Stress & Workload Manager**

**Background:** Uni students are juggling a lot at once with assignments, part-time jobs, social commitments, errands,
plus just keeping up physically and mentally. It’s rarely one big thing that causes burnout. It’s everything piling up
together without anyone noticing until it’s too late.

**The Problem:** Most students don’t really know how much they’re carrying at any given point, or which part of their
life is actually overloaded. So they keep saying yes to things, put off the stuff that feels less urgent, and end up
running on empty before they realise what happened.

**What The Solution Should Solve:** Build an app that gives students a clear picture of their load across different
areas (mental, time, physical, social, errands) and actually helps them do something about it before burnout hits. It
shouldn’t just track and report. Instead, it should help students rebalance what they’re carrying and push them toward
recovery, like rest or getting out of the house. Make sure it’s usable, accessible, and something students would
actually keep open on their phone.

**Examples Of Features:** For example, think of a workload visualiser showing load by category, with something like
"You’re at 90% capacity this week" as a quick sanity-check. A stress tracker to log how you’re feeling over time. A load
balancer that groups tasks together and pushes back lower-priority ones when things get hectic. And some kind of
recovery nudge, suggesting sleep, downtime, or a hangout when someone’s clearly overloaded. Teams are free to build any
features with any tech that fits.

### Planning An Escape

**Lifestyle Track: Planning An Escape**

**Travel Planner**

**Background:** Planning a trip means dealing with flights, places to stay, budgets, activities, and whatever everyone
in the group actually wants to do, and that’s before anything changes at the last minute. It’s a lot to hold together,
and it usually ends up scattered across five different apps and a group chat.

**The Problem:** Most travel apps only handle one piece of this with either bookings, or budgeting, or itineraries. So
travellers end up manually piecing it all together themselves. Group trips make it worse, since getting everyone’s
schedules, budgets, and preferences to line up is genuinely difficult. And when something changes mid-trip, there’s
rarely any real help from existing platforms in adjusting.

**What The Solution Should Solve:** Build something that helps people plan a trip from start to finish with budgeting,
building an itinerary, syncing preferences across a group, and adjusting plans when things don’t go as expected. It
should make planning faster and less stressful, and work well whether someone’s travelling solo or with a group.

**Examples Of Features:** Could include AI-generated itineraries based on budget and interests, tools for groups to
combine preferences and split costs, the ability to re-plan on the fly if a flight gets delayed or plans fall through,
and live pricing and availability through maps or booking APIs. Teams are free to use any tech stack to realise their
vision.

## General Stipulations

General stipulations covering platform and tech flexibility

1. Mobile applications may be developed for iOS only, Android only, or both. Teams are not required to support both
   platforms.
2. Mobile solutions may be built as native apps (Swift, Kotlin, Java, etc.) or using cross-platform frameworks (Flutter,
   React Native, etc.).
3. Where a problem statement says "Mobile Application and/or Website," teams may choose to build either one, or both. A
   web-only or mobile-only submission is acceptable.
4. Web-based solutions may be built as traditional websites or as browser-based apps. Responsive design for mobile
   browsers is encouraged.
5. Teams are free to use any tech stack, framework, or language they’re comfortable with.
6. Solutions must be deployable. Submissions should not rely solely on running in a local development environment as
   teams should be able to demonstrate the app running on an actual device, emulator, or hosted environment.
7. Use of third-party APIs, SDKs, or paid services is allowed, provided free-tier or trial access is available for demo
   purposes. Teams are responsible for their own API keys and usage limits.
8. Use of AI tools and APIs for development or as part of the solution itself is permitted where relevant but teams must
   be aware of the inner-workings of the projects they submit.
9. Teams may use pre-existing boilerplate, templates, or open-source libraries, but the core logic and features
   addressing the problem statement must be built during the hackathon.
10. Accessibility considerations (e.g. screen reader support, text-to-speech, colour contrast) are encouraged where
    mentioned in a problem statement, but partial implementation is acceptable given time constraints.
11. All submissions must be original work created during the hackathon period. Prior personal projects may not be
    resubmitted.
